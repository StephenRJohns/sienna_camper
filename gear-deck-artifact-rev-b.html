Capacity margins: cam straps WLL 400 lb each (two per item), stud fittings WLL 1,333 lb, L-track itself ≥2,000 lb/ft. The straps govern — 800 lb of working restraint on a 75 lb fridge covers a hard-stop surge with room to spare. Chocks and the surface-mounted track lips take sliding loads; straps take lift.

S2Anchor strategy

Primary anchors — the three third-row seat strikers (the wire loops you circled at the base of the second row). These are the latch points the third-row seat legs lock into when deployed; they carry FMVSS 207 crash loads and are the strongest exposed steel in the cargo floor. The deck's forward ratchet straps land here.

Secondary anchors — the two factory tie-down D-rings at the tailgate edge. Light stamped-wire convenience hooks, unrated by Toyota. Used here for anti-lift and anti-rattle only, never as primary restraint.

Forward surge path: the deck's front edge butts the raised floor step at the striker row, so in a hard stop the load path is wood-to-steel structure — the straps only keep the deck seated. Every appliance straps to deck-mounted L-track, never to the factory hooks.

Rails come as 48″ sticks (4-pack) — cut with a hacksaw or miter saw and deburr. The fourth stick stays whole as a spare, or cut it into two 12″ stubs screwed fore-aft along the side edges for extra side-pull points. Stud fittings drop in every 1″ along a rail, so fore-aft strap positions are infinitely adjustable — the fixed engineering is the three centerlines above. Bay depths are chock-snug on purpose: fridge (28.0″ per the BougeRV manual) in a 28.9″ clear bay pushed aft against Rail R, WAVE 3 (11.7″) in 12.4″. The ½″-tall track lip plus item snugness stops sliding; straps handle lift.

Rear bay width allocation — compressor plenum + fan duct. The fridge and kitchen together are 37.7″ wide, so the spare width is consolidated into one 2.0″ channel between them: fridge flush to the driver-side deck edge, channel, kitchen flush to the passenger edge. Per the BougeRV Rocky manual, the unit wants 100 mm (3.9″) at the sides and 200 mm (7.9″) at the back, with the compressor compartment, louvers, control panel, and power port all at one end. The layout meets this by pointing the compressor/control end forward — the DELTA stack in front of it is only 7.9″ deep against Rail F, leaving a ≈6″ open-air plenum (≈160 mm) between the stack and the fridge's hot end, open to the full cabin volume above. The fridge sits aft against Rail R (its lip is the aft chock) with the louvered long side facing the 2.0″ channel, which the MULTIFAN S1 runs as a forced-air duct, blowing aft and exhausting at the tailgate. The Govee sensor lives in the plenum facing the compressor grille, with an app alert at 110 °F. Run ECO mode when the van is closed up; crack a window or run the WAVE 3 if the alert trips. Trade-off of the forward-facing control panel: you read temps via the BougeRV app (manual §13) instead of the panel — the app is the everyday interface anyway.

S7Installation

  1. Template & verify

    Stow the third row flat. Lay cardboard over the floor and trace the usable outline: tailgate edge, side trim, and a front edge stopping 2″ behind the striker row so all three strikers stay exposed. Record measurements A, B, C from the callout above.

  2. Cut the deck

    Transfer the template to the plywood, cut, round all corners (~1″ radius), and ease the edges. Dry-fit in the van: it should drop in flat, butt the front floor step, and leave the tailgate latch striker clear.

  3. Mount the rails

    Snap chalk lines at the three centerlines (2.5″ / 16.0″ / 46.0″ from the front edge). Center each cut rail side-to-side, mark through the track's pre-drilled holes, and drill ¼″ through the deck. Counterbore the underside with a ¾″ Forstner bit ~⅛″ deep at each hole so the T-nuts sit flush, tap the T-nuts in, and bolt the rails down with the flat-head screws — snug plus a quarter turn (~75 in-lb).

  4. Finish

    Sand and seal with two coats of polyurethane (or glue down indoor/outdoor carpet, slitting it over the rails). Glue the 1×2 chock behind the kitchen-box position after a loaded dry-fit confirms its exact spot.

  5. Set the deck

    Lay the rubber mat on the folded seatbacks, then the deck on top. Drop two stud D-rings into Rail F near its ends and run the two ratchet straps forward and down to the outer seat strikers — tension until snug, not cranked. Drop two studs into Rail R ends and cam-strap down to the factory tie-downs.

  6. Load & strap the gear

    Heavy items go in their bays per DWG-01 — fridge and kitchen in the rear bay, WAVE 3 and power stack forward against Rail M / Rail F. Two cam straps over each item, stud ends into the flanking rails, buckles positioned where you can reach them from the tailgate.

    Fridge orientation: compressor/control end forward into the plenum, louvered side toward the vent channel, pushed aft against Rail R. The Rocky's lid hinge is reversible (manual §4.4) — set it so the lid swings toward the driver-side wall and you load groceries from the channel side while standing at the tailgate. First setup only: let the fridge rest 12 hours after transport before powering on (manual requirement).

  7. Wire & vent

    Fridge 12V cable → DELTA 3 Plus 12V port — with the fridge's power port now facing forward, it's a short direct run to the DELTA sitting right in front of it. WAVE 3 → DELTA AC outlet (or its own add-on battery). Route cables along deck edges, out of strap paths. Fit the window vent kit in the passenger-side rear window and connect the WAVE 3 exhaust duct — the front-passenger bay puts the unit a straight 2–3 ft run from the glass, which matters: every added foot of exhaust hose and every 90° bend adds static-pressure loss the compressor has to fight, and a long hot hose radiates heat back into the van.

    Vent gear: zip-tie the MULTIFAN S1 to a Rail M stud fitting at the front mouth of the 2″ channel, blowing aft along the fridge's louvered side (USB power from a DELTA port; low speed is enough for continuous duty). Stick the Govee sensor to the back of the DELTA stack facing the fridge's compressor grille across the plenum, and set a Govee app alert at 110 °F.

    If you later hardwire any 12V runs (instead of the factory plugs), terminate them in Anderson Powerpole SB50 (50–75 A) or XT60/XT90 (60–90 A) connectors rather than screw terminals — silver/gold-plated contacts hold <1 mΩ across hundreds of unplug cycles, so the pull-it-out-in-a-minute goal doesn't create loose, heat-generating connections. The factory EcoFlow and fridge cables are already quick-disconnect; custom connectors are only needed for added circuits.

  8. Road test

    Drive 15–30 minutes with normal braking and a few firm stops in an empty lot. Re-tension every strap afterward — new webbing settles. Then run the pre-drive checklist below before every trip.

S8Pre-drive checklist & limits

  • All 8 item straps tensioned, cam buckles fully closed, loose tails wrapped
  • Deck anchor straps snug — 2 forward at strikers, 2 aft at factory rings
  • Fridge lid latched; kitchen box locked closed; slide not deployed
  • WAVE 3 ducts either stowed or vent panel seated in window
  • No strap, cable, or duct across the tailgate latch; tailgate closes freely
  • Nothing loose on top of strapped items
Limits

Third row is unusable while the deck is in (it sits on the folded seatbacks) — full removal is 4 straps and a slide-out. Never substitute the factory tailgate-edge hooks for primary restraint. Keep total deck cargo under ~350 lb and the heaviest items forward, against Rail M or the front bay. Re-check strap tension after the first 30 minutes of any trip.

S9Spare tire — RJ-MODINI in-cabin kit

The Sienna carries no factory spare; the RJ-MODINI kit (T155/85R18 on an 18×4 steel wheel, 28.5″ rolling diameter × ~6″ wide, tool case nesting flush in the wheel barrel, jack and covers — call it ~45 lb as one package) rides in the cabin. It does not fit on the deck: laid flat on the kitchen box, a 28.5″ disc overhangs the 20″-wide box by ~6″ into the fridge (which stands 4″ taller) or past the deck edge into the wall trim.

Stowage: the third-row footwell, forward of the deck. The strip of open floor between the seat-striker row and the second-row seatbacks is dead space the deck never claims. Stand the tire upright there (28.5″ tall ≈ seatback height), leaning against the second-row seatbacks, centered on the van, in its soft cover. Run one cam strap through the wheel hub down to the center seat striker — the deck's forward anchors use only the two outer strikers, so the crash-rated center one is free. That puts 45 lb at the lowest, most central spot in the cargo area (the best place in the whole van for it) and touches nothing on the deck.

Checks: verify the second row still has the slide/recline range you use with the tire in place, and that the strap is tight enough that the tire can't drum against the seatback. Getting the tire out at a flat means opening a sliding door, not the tailgate — arguably faster than unloading a deck bay anyway. Keep the jack's tool case nested in the wheel barrel so it's all one strapped mass.
